# Skip the Resume Rush, Sell Compliance Paperwork

The best unmet "upload a file → get a finished file" businesses are not in résumés, chat-with-PDF or cover letters. Those are crowded: Rezi alone makes about **$249K a month** and nearly every rival has added AI. The open money is in jobs where small businesses and stressed individuals **already pay a human per file** to turn messy documents into something an authority, buyer or platform will accept, and where no polished AI product exists yet. Ranked by demand × willingness to pay × competition gap, the strongest picks are: **PDF accessibility fixing** for US public bodies (agencies charge $5–$12 a page, and legal deadlines run to 2027–2028); **site-specific safety plans and HACCP food-safety plans** for micro-firms (consultants charge $1,500–$5,000 each); **Indian government tender (GeM) bid packs** (₹10,000 buys about 7 bids from consultants, and no AI tool turned up); and **O-1/EB-1A visa evidence packs** (law firms charge $7,000–$15,000). High-value US consumer claim packets (home insurance, disability appeals) also pay well and have no AI rival, but legal risk is high and they are hard to sell from India. For a solo builder in India, the safest first bets are accessibility fixing (global buyers, no legal advice involved, and a human-review step that suits an Indian cost base) and GeM bid packs (home market, weekly repeat use). One caution runs through all of this: most "open gap" labels come from one search per idea, so run a proper competitor sweep before you build.

## The résumé idea and eight other categories are already crowded

Your résumé example is the clearest case of a crowded market. Reviewers say nearly every major résumé platform added AI in 2026. Teal, Rezi, Kickresume, Enhancv, Resume.io, Jobscan, VisualCV and Zety all sell at about **$20–$30 a month** ([VisualCV](https://www.visualcv.com/blog/best-ai-resume-builders/); [macmyths](https://macmyths.com/best-ai-resume-builders-in-2026-rezi-teal-kickresume-and-more/)). The leader is strong and mostly self-funded. Rezi shows a Stripe-verified **$248,550 monthly revenue from 10,312 paying subscribers** and $10.34M in all-time revenue ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rezi)). Teal made **$4.2M in 2024** and raised a $7.5M Series A ([JustAINews](https://justainews.com/?p=3167); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/teal-1)). A newcomer starts very small. One indie AI résumé site went from **$15 to $141 a month** in its first three months and took about half a year to pass $1K ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/my-ai-resume-maker-website-passes-$1k-in-mrr)). There is also pushback from buyers: almost one in five HR managers reject applications that look fully AI-written ([Zemith](https://www.zemith.com/de/contents/how-to-use-ai-for-job-search-2026)). Adding keywords and polishing descriptions is now a standard feature, not a business.

The same is true for several other "obvious" ideas. Chat-with-PDF is a commodity at $8–$20 a month. One PDF-chat app with 100K+ users made only about **$6,500 in a month** ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/chatpdf); [Acquire.com](https://app.acquire.com/startup/bhupho77jk-chat-with-pdfs-and-note-taking-profitable-ai-saas-loved-by-100k-users)). Flight-delay claims belong to AirHelp and its rivals, which take a 30% cut ([Travelspill](https://travelspill.com/?p=4737)). Grant writing has Grantable, Instrumentl, Granted AI and GrantCopilot at $20–$500 a month ([Grantable](https://grantable.co/guides/ai-grant-writing-tools); [GrantCopilot](https://www.grantcopilot.ai/blog/best-grant-writing-software-2026)). Bank statement → spreadsheet is served by DocuClipper at about $0.05 a page, and in India by Suvit, which has ICAI backing ([DocuClipper](https://www.docuclipper.com/pricing); [ICAI](https://bs.icai.org/suvit-2/)). Roofing insurance supplements gained at least six AI entrants in a short time ([Roofing Software Guide](https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/guides/how-to-write-roofing-supplements-with-software/)). Prior authorisation has free tools such as CoverMyMeds ([Health AI Daily](https://healthaidaily.com/blog/best-ai-prior-authorization-software-small-practices-2026/)). Lease summaries cost $10–$25 per lease from self-serve tools ([LeaseCodex](https://leasecodex.com/lease-abstraction-cost/)). Layout-preserving PDF translation gives away 1,000 free pages a month ([BabelDOC](https://pypi.org/project/BabelDOC/)). Construction submittals now face Procore itself ([Datagrid](https://datagrid.com/agents/for/build-submittal-packages)). Study-abroad SOPs have no dedicated AI leader, but the price ceiling is low (₹600–₹1,500 on Freelancer.com), free chatbots do the job, and universities run similarity checks ([Freelancer](https://www.freelancer.ie/projects/research-writing/australia-graduate-engineering-study); [vocal.media](https://vocal.media/education/things-to-know-before-choosing-the-best-sop-writing-services-in-india)).

Résumés still have a few gaps, but they are small. Country-format CVs (Europass, German Lebenslauf) sell for about $50 a gig on Fiverr ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/m_baqir786110/make-europass-resume-cv)). Gulf Arabic–English CVs already have five or more small tools, one at **$2.99 per résumé** ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2113794/resumify)). No naira-priced AI CV tool turned up for Nigeria, while Kenya already has M-Pesa-priced options around KSh 1,700 ([ProGigFinder](https://www.progigfinder.com/cv-writing-services-kenya)). These are thin, low-price niches, not a way into the main résumé market.

## Money is easiest to find where people already pay a human per file

The winning pattern across all four sets of research notes has three parts. First, a human already charges per job. Second, the output must pass a known standard, rule or validator, such as OSHA, HACCP, WCAG/PDF-UA, a tender notice or a KDP upload check. Third, the buyer is too small for enterprise software. When all three hold, an AI draft priced at **10–30% of the human fee** looks like a bargain to the buyer and still leaves room for margin. The standard also protects you from "just paste it into ChatGPT," because a chatbot cannot promise the file will pass the check.

## Five picks that a solo builder can actually win

**PDF accessibility fixing is the strongest all-round bet.** US public bodies must make their web content, including PDFs, meet WCAG 2.1 AA. The Justice Department pushed the deadline to **April 2027 for entities serving 50,000+ people and April 2028 for smaller ones**, but did not change what is required ([Plante Moran](https://plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-web-accessibility-compliance-deadlines)). That gives small towns and school districts a long buying window with limited budgets. Agencies charge **$7.50–$11.50 a page** (Accessible.org) and $5–$8 at Allyant, so a 50-page document costs $250–$600 ([Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/pricing/); [Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost/)). The cheapest self-serve rival found, Fruition, charges $0.50–$2 a page, and the AI leader PREP is quote-only with no free plan ([Fruition](https://www.fruition.net/ai-platforms/pdf-accessibility); [iTechGuides](https://www.itechguides.com/products/prep/)). Much of the hard work is open source: opendataloader-pdf (29.7K GitHub stars) does layout analysis and auto-tagging, but keeps certified PDF/UA export behind a paid add-on ([README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/opendataloader-project/opendataloader-pdf@main/README.md)). The catch is quality. Automated checkers catch only **20–30% of issues**, the DOJ itself cited "limits of generative AI for remediation," and the PDF Association says the best results come from AI tagging plus human review ([Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost/); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ada-title-ii-web-accessibility-3432173/); [PDF Association](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/0-3-16_15-AnnKnettler-Beyond_Automation.pdf)). That catch helps a builder in India: offer AI tagging plus a human checker at $1–$3 a page, deliver a compliance report with each file, and you undercut agencies by 70%+ while keeping a moat that pure software cannot copy.

**GeM tender bid packs are the best home-market opening.** Every government bid needs the same stack of papers checked against the tender notice: GST, PAN, Udyam, Class III digital signature, three years of audited accounts, a solvency certificate, experience certificates and power of attorney, plus MSME exemption claims ([TenderFlowPro](https://tenderflowpro.in/blog/complete-government-tender-guide-india-2026)). Consultants charge **₹10,000 for 7 bids** or ₹15,000 packages ([VakilAdda](https://vakiladda.com/gem-registration-and-tender-consultant/); [LegalAdda](https://legaladda.com/gem-tender-bidding-consultant/)). Search found only human consultancies and tender-listing portals, with no AI tool that reads the tender, checks the firm's eligibility and assembles an ordered, checklist-backed bid ([Tender18](https://tender18.com/gem-registration)). Active bidders bid often, so this suits a monthly plan around ₹499–₹999 per bid or a flat monthly fee. Treat the "open gap" as unconfirmed until you search GeM seller forums and Product Hunt properly.

**Safety plans and HACCP plans turn a $2,000 consultant job into a $200 product.** US general contractors require a site-specific safety plan from each subcontractor before work starts. Consultants charge **$1,500–$5,000 per project** and take about a week. The cheapest generator, CrewCompliance, charges $149, calls itself "not a safety consulting firm," and does not read the actual project documents ([Greenberg Safety](https://greenbergsafety.com/blog/construction-safety-consultant-cost); [CrewCompliance](https://crewcompliance.org/pricing); [Evolution Safety](https://evolutionsafetyresources.com/site-specific-safety-plan-contents-what-to-include/)). HACCP plans look similar: **$1,500–$5,000** from consultants, $3,250+ at some firms, while most software ($24–$250 a month) only handles daily logs and does not write the plan ([Xenia](https://www.xenia.team/articles/how-to-write-a-haccp-plan); [Finley](https://www.finleyfbconsulting.com/haccp); [Audit-Binder](https://www.audit-binder.com/blog/haccp-plan-cost)). HACCP has a wider market than SSSP because it also applies in the EU, UK and India, which lets you test at home first. Both carry real liability if a plan misses a hazard, so sell "AI draft plus optional paid expert sign-off," not a finished guarantee.

**O-1/EB-1A evidence packs sit next to a $10,000 price tag.** Visa firms charge flat fees of **$10,000 (Lighthouse O-1), $11,500 (O-1 agent), $15,000 (EB-1A)**, and $6,999–$12,500 at Manifest Law ([Lighthouse](https://www.lighthousehq.com/pricing); [Beyond Border](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/alma-alternatives)). Every player found is attorney-led. Boundless pairs lawyers and ex-USCIS officers "with AI," but no self-serve tool takes a CV plus evidence and returns a criteria map, exhibit index and draft petition letter ([Boundless](https://www.boundless.com/partnerships/partners-eb-1a-experts)). Indian founders and researchers in your own network are a natural first market to test this with. The legal risk is serious: preparing someone's immigration case can count as practising law without a licence. The safer models are a "self-prep organiser" that tells users to have a lawyer file, or a white-label tool sold to immigration lawyers.

**KDP book formatting is the low-risk practice run.** Self-published authors pay Fiverr sellers **$10–$85 per book** for a print-ready PDF plus EPUB, and a whole gig niche exists just to "fix KDP formatting errors" ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/quill_echo/format-your-kdp-paperback-and-ebook-interior-for-amazon); [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/stacey_donlad/fix-kdp-formatting-errors-book-formatting-for-amazon-kdp-paperback-formatting)). The software options are all do-it-yourself: Kindle Create is free but basic, Atticus costs $147, and Vellum costs $199.99–$249.99 and runs only on Mac ([Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/atticus-vs-vellum/); [publishing.co.uk](https://www.publishing.co.uk/guides/atticus-vs-vellum-vs-kindle-create/)). A "send .docx, get files that pass KDP's checker" service at $15–$29 a book carries no legal risk and no regulated advice. The ticket is small, so treat it as a fast first product rather than the main business.

## High-value US claim packets pay well but carry legal danger

The US consumer side shows the largest pain and the highest fees, but it also carries the most legal risk. Health insurers on HealthCare.gov denied about **one in five in-network claims**, and patients appealed **fewer than 1%** of them ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2025/01/insurance-denial-marketplace/)). Patients already use ChatGPT and Claude to fight bills, and the American Hospital Association warned its members about the trend ([Bioethics.com](https://bioethics.com/?p=102143)). Willingness to pay is weak, though. Counterforce Health is free for patients and Claimable (about $40 a letter) is moving toward selling to drugmakers and hospitals ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce); [beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.beehiiv.com/p/71-of-health-insurers-are-using-ai-to-decide-your-claims)). Huge demand plus free rivals makes this a poor fit for a small paid product.

The bigger tickets are disability and home-insurance claims. Lawyers take 25% of disability back pay, and representation raises the first-stage award rate from about **one-third to more than half**, which shows that better paperwork changes outcomes ([CRR Boston College](https://crr.bc.edu/?p=24184)). Public adjusters take **10–20%** of home-insurance claims, and search found no AI consumer tool for either case ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/public-insurance-adjusters)). The DoNotPay case shows the danger. The company started with parking appeals, then the FTC finalised an order in February 2025 with $193K in relief and barred it from unsupported "robot lawyer" claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/DoNotPayInc-Complaint.pdf); [Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/legal-innovation-center/2024/09/25/chatbot-law-site-donotpay-settles-with-ftc/)). Lawyers also warn that AI deposit-demand letters can backfire by citing the wrong law ([terms.law](https://terms.law/2025/02/24/why-ai-generated-security-deposit-demand-letters-can-backfire/)). Selling these from India means US marketing, US trust and US legal exposure all at once. Leave them for later, or sell the tool to the lawyers and adjusters themselves.

UK parking appeals are the cleanest consumer "letter wins money" case. The appeals service POPLA handled a record **107,202 appeals** in a year and cancelled **50.5%**, with 39,522 not even contested by the operator ([POPLA](https://www.popla.co.uk/news/over-100000-motorists-appeal-private-parking-charges)). Tickets are cheap and forums help for free, so this works only as a low-price, high-volume product. Trust in the category is damaged after DoNotPay.

## India's own paperwork gives a home-field edge, but crowds form fast

Indian notice replies prove people will pay, and they also show how quickly a gap closes. GST Reply AI charges **₹299 per reply or ₹999 a month unlimited for CA firms** ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/gst-reply-ai/gst-reply-ai)). By late 2026 it had at least four rivals: Notice Sahayak, TaxNoticeAI, NoticeAI (listed by ICAI) and Jamku ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/notice-sahayak); [ICAI](https://ai.icai.org/usecases_details.php?id=320)). Human CA fees of ₹1,500–₹15,000 per reply leave room for more players, but a new entrant needs a different angle: WhatsApp delivery, regional-language replies, or white-label tools for CA firms ([GST Reply AI](https://gstreplyai.com/gst-notice-reply-ai-vs-ca)). Income-tax notices for salaried people look a little more open. Search found mostly human CA services and one Hindi AI page, but TaxNoticeAI and Notice Sahayak already reach into this space ([Richify.ai](https://www.richify.ai/in/hi/income-tax-notice); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/taxnoticeai)).

Hindi and regional-language rent agreements and legal notices have only free template sites and hackathon projects so far ([Vakeel360](https://vakeel360.com/legal-tools); [lablab](https://lablab.ai/submissions/v6h4n54vzr69ms5cutwntdb5)). The volume is high, but the price per document is low, so it works best as a WhatsApp-first, UPI-paid product. Cataloguing for Flipkart and Meesho also looks open. Agencies charge ₹1,000 for up to 50 products, then ₹20 per product, and no "photo + bill → bulk catalogue file" tool turned up. That gap was not searched deeply, so it is not confirmed ([IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/product-listing-service-on-amazon-flipkart-meesho-25496407488.html)).

Outside India, the largest raw volume is Mexico's **23.2 million SAT tax letters** in fiscal 2025. Current tools serve accountants, not the person who got the letter ([DPL News](https://dplnews.com/no-mas-temor-al-sat-siigo-lanza-herramienta-de-cumplimiento-y-conciliacion-fiscal/)). Language and tax-law knowledge make it a weak fit for you. Germany shows a warning sign: accounting-software firms give away "explain my tax-office letter" tools for free to attract customers ([Norman Finance](https://norman.finance/de/tools/finanzamt-brief)). Expect the same to happen to any "explain this letter" product. People pay for the finished reply, not the explanation.

## Price per job, undercut the human, and add a pro tier

The pricing evidence points to one model. Give the first file or a free check away. Charge per document for one-off pain, priced at **50–90% below the human agent**. Add a monthly unlimited plan for professionals who handle many files, such as CAs, safety consultants, visa lawyers or accessibility firms, because that is where recurring revenue comes from. GST Reply AI does exactly this ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/gst-reply-ai/gst-reply-ai)). For money-recovery products, a success fee well below AirHelp's 30% or an adjuster's 10–20% is the natural pitch. Expect low conversion: Rezi's roughly 1M new users a year against about 10,300 paying subscribers works out to around **1% paid**, which is my own calculation from its page ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rezi)). Plan for that with SEO and free tools that bring people in, which is how the indie résumé site grew ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/my-ai-resume-maker-website-passes-$1k-in-mrr)).

Regional rules matter. For Indian buyers, per-document prices around ₹199–₹1,999 fit the evidence (₹299 for a GST reply, ₹2,000+ for a human SOP writer), paid through local methods. African buyers pay through mobile money such as M-Pesa and Paystack ([ProGigFinder](https://www.progigfinder.com/cv-writing-services-kenya); [theaireport](https://www.theaireport.ai/tooldatabase/careergo)). US and EU B2B buyers accept per-page or per-plan pricing in dollars or euros, and they want a report or audit trail they can show a regulator. Privacy is part of what people buy for personal and tax files. Tax preparers warn against putting return data into public AI, and people with personal archives prefer local-first tools ([findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/tax-preparers-cp2000-ai-workflow/); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40528192)). A clear "no training, auto-delete in 24 hours" promise costs almost nothing and helps you win.

The main risks rank like this. First is **legal liability**: anything that looks like legal, tax, immigration or safety advice can bring unlicensed-practice claims or harm users if it is wrong, as DoNotPay's FTC order shows. Second is **wrong output**: chatbots "confidently invent" billing rules ([findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/dispute-medical-bill-with-ai/)), so cite real codes and sections and keep a human review tier. Third is **free competition**, from general chatbots and from vertical software firms using free tools as lead magnets. Fourth is **thin evidence**: none of these notes found direct Reddit or forum quotes of people saying "I would pay," and several "open gap" labels rest on one search each. Copyright (scanned books) and plagiarism checks (SOPs) are smaller risks specific to those ideas.

## Conclusion

The question "what file service do people want but cannot buy?" mostly has the wrong shape. Generic improvement of a document (résumé polish, summaries, chat) is already free or cheap everywhere. The money sits in **acceptance**: a file that a contractor, regulator, tender portal, publisher or visa officer will accept. Those buyers pay for the result, not the writing, and a validator or rulebook stands between them and ChatGPT. That moat favours a builder who will learn one dull rulebook deeply over one who wraps a model in a nice upload page.

For a solo builder in India, the practical move is to start where an Indian cost base is an advantage instead of a handicap. PDF accessibility fixing with AI plus human review, sold to small US public bodies before the 2027–2028 deadlines, combines a legal mandate, a high per-page anchor and no regulated advice. GeM bid packs give a home-market product with weekly repeat use. KDP formatting can be your two-week warm-up to learn sales. Before writing code for any of them, spend a day on a real competitor sweep (Product Hunt, There's An AI For That, the buyer's own forums) and talk to five people who paid a human for this job in the last year. A gap that survives that test is worth building.
